02 — Shading · Scale Detail

Per-Scale Gradient

Every individual dorsal scale has its own internal shading. The darkest point is at the bottom edge of each scale — where it sits beneath the scale above it (cast shadow from overlap). Moving up toward the free tip, it lightens.

This is repeated consistently across the entire body. The result is a "roof tile" depth effect — each scale reads as a 3D object, not just a drawn outline. This is what gives Bayun's snakes their dimensional, almost sculptural quality.

The belly (ventral) surface uses a completely different texture: tight horizontal parallel bands — like compressed ribs — rather than scales. Each band carries its own shadow on the lower edge. The transition from dorsal scales to ventral plates is a defined anatomical line.

02 — Shading · Body Shadow

Hard Shadow Cuts

ref-leg-red-cord.jpg

Deep black in coil folds and shadow sides — note the hard cut, not a gradual fade

The cylindrical snake body has a highlight zone and a shadow zone. The shadow side drops to deep black quickly — not a long soft gradient. A relatively hard edge where the shadow begins.

Where the body curves away from view, overlaps itself, or tucks into a coil fold, it goes to full black saturation. No muddy midtones. No soft airbrush blend.

The tonal range is achieved entirely through line density and scale shading — not grey ink or soft blending. Where it reads as "grey," it's fine lines and dot clusters. This is what keeps the piece crisp and readable as it ages.

The contrast is stark and intentional: deep black vs. precise fine lines. Nothing in between.

02 — Shading · Coil Logic

The Coil Twist Shadow

When the snake body bends into a loop or twist, the curve itself generates the lighting — not an external light source.

The inside of the curve (the tightest part of the bend) drops to near-pure black — a hard, confident shadow shape. The apex of the curve (where the body faces outward) is the lightest point, with scale texture fully visible. The sides transition quickly from apex to shadow.

These inner-curve blacks become abstract shapes in their own right — bold dark voids that add visual weight and drama to every coil. They're not just shadows — they're compositional elements.

The twist is the shading. The bend tells you where the dark goes.

02 — Shading · Head

Head Anatomy

The head uses the same per-scale shading logic as the body, but the scales are larger and more individually prominent — each one more visible.

Eye: a simple, clean circle — small, round dark pupil, no elaborate iris rendering. Quiet.

Important: closed mouth. No open jaw for this piece. The snake is calm — not a strike pose. This is one of the biggest departures from Bayun's other work (shown right). The energy should be powerful and self-contained, not aggressive.

If a tongue is present: two ultra-fine lines, trailing softly. Almost not there.

03 — Composition · Silhouette

The Tapered Diamond Silhouette

The overall bounding shape of the piece is an elongated diamond — wide at the top where the leg is widest (hip/quad), narrowing to a point at the ankle.

This mirrors the natural reverse taper of the leg itself. The silhouette of the composition and the silhouette of the body become the same shape. The piece belongs to the leg rather than sitting on top of it.

The width of the coil spread grows proportionally as the leg gets wider going up — this is what Bayun got right and what Bobby needs to preserve.

03 — Composition · Geometry

Triangle + Head Direction

annotated-triangle-infinity.jpg

The upper coils near the hip form a secondary triangle shape. Inside it, the snake's head is positioned to follow the natural diagonal of the oblique muscles. The head direction isn't arbitrary — it's anatomically intentional.

There's also a secondary triangle formed by the infinity knot and the neck area — interesting geometry that creates visual rhythm across the piece.

The piece reads as placed by the body, not placed on it.

03 — Composition · Motion

The Möbius Effect

The snake's head overlaps its own body. This is a specific, intelligent choice. It creates a Möbius strip or infinite loop quality — follow the snake's motion and you never find a beginning or end. The flow is continuous.

It makes the piece feel alive in a way that a snake with a clear start and end point doesn't. The eye keeps moving.

Combined with the top of the snake curling back on itself as it rises toward the ribs — the whole piece has a self-referential, looping energy that never resolves. That's the intention.

03 — Composition · Negative Space

Infinity Knot + Negative Ring Space

annotated-negative-space.jpg

A rich variety of negative space sizes — each one designed, not incidental

The lower-mid section contains a clear figure-8 infinity knot — a designed shape, not just a coil. It's a visual and conceptual anchor of the piece.

Throughout the piece, large ring shapes create open negative space voids. These are designed shapes — as intentional as the snake itself. The circles of skin inside the coils carry visual weight. A rich variety of negative space sizes creates contrast and interest across the composition.

The big sweeping ring on the quad was specifically requested — that open skin is not leftover space.

03 — Composition · Flow

Consistent Directional Zig-Zag

The snake's overall motion follows a consistent upward zig-zag — not random, not chaotic. The zig-zag size increases proportionally as the leg gets wider going up. At the top, the composition goes straight up to align with the hip transition.

Breathing room is maintained at the front edge — the snake never crowds the boundary. This keeps it from feeling forced.

The comparison image (right) shows coherent directional flow on the left side vs. directionless chaos on the right side. The left is always the goal.
